A guide from plan to care

Health insurance, step by step.

A health plan helps share the cost of eligible care. Here’s the usual path, from comparing your options to understanding a bill.

See how the steps fit together ↓
Your care, made clearer
Think of it as a partnership between you, your plan, and your care providers.

You pay a premium to keep the policy active. When you receive care, the plan applies its coverage rules and shares eligible costs. Your exact benefits, network, and costs depend on the policy you choose.

The journey

Four steps. A clearer picture.

Each plan works a little differently, but these are the main things to expect. Check your plan documents for the specific rules and amounts that apply to you.

01

Choose a plan

Compare the monthly premium, deductible, copayments, out-of-pocket maximum, and provider network. Think about your care needs, prescriptions, and budget.

02

Pay your premium

Your premium is the regular amount you pay to keep coverage active. You may also pay a deductible or a share of costs when you use covered services.

03

Get the care you need

When you need care, check whether the provider is in your plan’s network and whether the service needs a referral or prior approval. Preventive care may have different cost rules.

04

Understand the bill

Your provider usually sends a claim to the insurer. The plan processes it and explains what it paid and what you may owe. Review the explanation of benefits, then compare it with the provider’s bill.

A few useful terms

Know what you may pay.

Premium: what you pay regularly to keep your plan active. Deductible: what you pay for certain covered services before the plan starts sharing costs.

Copayment or coinsurance: your share of the cost for a covered service. Out-of-pocket maximum: the yearly limit on what you pay for covered services, as defined by your plan.

Coverage, costs, provider networks, and eligibility vary by plan and location. This is a general guide, not a quote or a guarantee of benefits. Review your plan documents and contact your insurer with questions about a specific service or bill.
